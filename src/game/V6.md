## Premise (replaces 1957)
The near future, humanity a level beyond today. The **United Nations of Antarctica and the Americas (UNAA)** runs a
research program to colonise alien planets with smart robot bases. You are its AI assistant: **UNIT 07**, created by
the program's lead scientist. You start as a small floating ball robot on the research ship. On a planet you become a
big **stationary pod** with arms; you cannot move, but you build arms, limbs, drones and machines (all costly), and
your workers are **creatures you engineer to the max and equip with robotic parts**. Rivals: other **future human
blocs** with their own robot colonies (no real nations as caricatures).

## Decisions (asked and answered)
- ART: keep the realistic look we have, but 100x better. Learn from wackyblocks (sketchfab.com/wackyblocks: dense
  hard-surface detail, panel lines, stencils and decals, tiny lights, layered greebles, edge wear and highlights, rich
  textures, beautiful soft light) WITHOUT going blocky. Low poly where it helps, high-resolution textures, realistic
  materials and proportions, stylised shading. More textured everywhere. Furniture and props: Poly Haven (CC0, API
  reachable: api.polyhaven.com), the user's Limbophobia (src/limbo/content/furniture) and Lerfolese
  (/home/user/pukkingdragon123/lerfolese/assets/models, CC0 Poly Haven) projects, and new models built with the user's
  lowpix tool (/home/user/pukkingdragon123/symmetrical-goggles). Everything restyled to fit. No Sketchfab downloads.
- UI: keep the green machine UI (Fallout-style phosphor on worn machine hardware), rebuilt in far more detail:
  hand-painted high-resolution pixel art plus detailed 3D where it helps, interactive, never messy, high-resolution
  fonts only, no filler text. Desktop first, at Steam-game quality (mouse and keyboard; phone still works).
- SHIP HUB: the ship is the main menu and the hub for every function. Clickable rooms in an isometric cutaway (no walking).
- PLANET CONTROL: the pod is fixed; you act through arms, steerable drones and the crew; workers walk.
- TEAMMATES: 2 crew come down with the pod; both die in a scripted story event.
- TIME: real-time with pause and 1x/2x/3x; orders can be given while paused.
- PROGRESSION: a MASTERY skill tree (paths: Plant Engineering, Bio Engineering, Energy Engineering, Full Industrial,
  Robotics; each with sub-specialties) + RESEARCH (learn by scanning and studying) + BUILD-TO-UNLOCK (e.g. you must
  build an Upgrader to install your own upgrades; find resources; install limbs or structures like a gravity pole).
- WORKERS: engineered creatures fitted with robotic parts (cyborg workers), linked to containers and machines.
- LOSE: colony collapse (power, oxygen, food or workers fail), pod destroyed, or mission recalled (missed quotas).
- ITEMS are real things, not currency: wood, leaves, fibre, resin, fruit, seeds, specimens, biomass, dirt, soil, clay,
  sand, gravel, rocks, minerals and ores, water and fluids, refined goods, parts. They live in real CONTAINERS.
- BUILD UI: Techtonica-style (big category tabs, item tiles with recipes and costs, hotbar quick-slots).
- NODE MAP: a wired node graph that controls REAL logistics (nodes = buildings, containers, deposits, fields; wires =
  routes with item filters, priorities and conditions; workers carry it out). Deep, and learned gradually.
- Game should be HARD: colony management, workers, random cinematic choice events.
- Publish once, after everything is built.

## Lead updates (user feedback after kickoff; binding)
- FONTS (user-chosen): ALL text uses **Departure Mono** (family 'Body'; crisp ONLY at 11, 22, 33 or 44 px) and ALL
  titles and headings use **Spleen** (family 'Title' = 12x24 at 24 or 48 px; 'TitleL' = 16x32 at 32 or 64 px;
  'TitleXL' = 32x64 at 64 px). The old family names in style.css are now aliases of these. Never use any other font,
  never size text off those multiples, never smooth it (no fractional scale, no blur, no CSS transforms that resample text).
- The user's verdict on the current UI, lab and pixel art: "ugly". Every UI element is hand-painted high-resolution
  pixel-art HARDWARE (worn machine panels, screws, seams, scratches, phosphor glass with scanlines and glow, physical
  switches and dials), never flat boxes or CSS rectangles with text. Interiors are cinematic film stills, every surface
  pixel-art textured, matching the user's Limbophobia game (src/limbo/*: gfx/materials.ts, gfx/pixel.ts, textures,
  world/objects.ts, content/*).
- **UI DIRECTION CHANGED (user, newest; supersedes "keep the green machine UI"):** "no need for monitor and green
  stuff; cooler, like for a plant show the 3D plant environment growing; game and UI more cool, fun and creative." The
  user chose **LIVING 3D VIGNETTES**:
  - Every panel's hero is a small LIVE 3D diorama of the thing itself:

    - a creature moves in its habitat;
    - a cyborg worker does its job;
    - a container opens to show its real contents;
    - a machine runs;
    - an item turns on a little plinth;
    - a planet spins under scan;
    - research shows the specimen under the scanner.
  - Stats appear as hand-drawn callouts with leader lines pointing AT parts of the 3D thing. Icons beat words; text is
    sparse.
  - Interactions are physical and playful: drag a seed into the soil, drop a part onto the creature, pour ore into a
    crate.
  - Frames are minimal HAND-DRAWN pixel-art strokes (irregular inked outlines, a little tape, pins and stamps) so the
    vignette is the star.
  - NO CRT monitors, NO green phosphor, NO scanlines, NO consoles-as-UI, NO dark boxy panels with lists.
  - Desktop first, never messy: one focused vignette at a time, with smooth camera and transition moves.
- **UI = DETAILED 3D, REAL TECH, WOW (user, newest; overrides the 2D-default rule):** "UI looks lazy and untextured,
  unacceptable. Focus on wow factor and use 3D, more detailed UI, cooler, impressive, every single UI, especially the
  tech, more realistic like real tech, and not complex, not an over-complicated game." The user chose MIX BY SCREEN:
  - SHIP AND COLONY FUNCTIONS are detailed 3D HARDWARE CONSOLES: real lab and control-room devices with glass screens
    (reflections, depth, slight curvature), machined bezels, lit buttons and keys that physically press, knobs, toggle
    switches, cables and vents, lit by the scene. The camera moves into the device.
  - SHOWCASES (tech and research, the planet, the creature) are realistic HOLOGRAMS projected above detailed hardware
    bases: spinning 3D models of the actual tech, building, planet or creature, with scanlines kept subtle, volumetric
    light from the emitter and particle shimmer.
  - Textures 32 px/m, clean and stylised (the hand-drawn ban still holds). Everything lit and shaded in 3D: no flat 2D
    rectangles as the main look.
  - SIMPLE: few, big choices per screen; icons and short labels; no grids of 16 tiles with costs.
  - SIMPLIFY (user-chosen): RESEARCH offers 3–4 techs at a time (pick one); MASTERY has fewer, clearer perks (about 5 or
    6 per path); FEWER SCREENS: research, mastery, the Upgrader and mission merge into ONE "R&D" station, with fewer
    tabs and buttons everywhere.
- **HAND-DRAWN IS BANNED (user, newest, overrides every earlier "hand-drawn" or "strokes" note):** "Never ever use
  hand-drawn visuals, I hate it so much. Textures lower resolution, more stylised. The wheels etc. should feel like a
  cool OBJECT, a tool with gears." So:
  - NO hand-drawn or sketchy look anywhere: no inked callouts, no chalk lines, no wobbly outlines, no brush strokes, no
    doodles, no pencil, no yarn-and-tape craft look, no lowpix `strokes` finish. Use lowpix `flat` (clean pixel art)
    and lower-resolution styles.
  - TEXTURES are LOWER RESOLUTION and MORE STYLISED (user-chosen density: **32 px per metre** on 3D surfaces; 2D UI
    art painted at low resolution and shown at a whole-number scale): chunky, clean pixel clusters, clear readable shapes, limited ramps
    from palette.ts. No busy photo noise.
  - UI elements are cool physical OBJECTS and TOOLS: machined metal, gears and cogs that turn, bezels, knurled knobs,
    levers, toggle switches, engraved and stamped plates, lenses, pistons. The tool wheel is a real mechanical device
    (a geared revolver drum or turret that clicks round). Gauges are real dials with needles. Things move mechanically
    when used.
  - Leader lines and callouts, if needed at all, are clean straight machined pointers or engraved tags, never hand-inked.
- **UI REFINED (user, newest; refines the 3D vignettes rule):** "Forget the chest UI. I want detailed 2D pixel-art UI,
  not always 3D, but detailed 2D. The paper needs to be textured, and not always paper, and no text slop. Be smart and
  polish, and make the game beautiful." So:
  - The DEFAULT UI is richly detailed, hand-drawn **2D pixel art** at high resolution, painted with lowpix strokes.
  - MATERIALS fit the context and are always textured, never flat fills:
    - paper with fibre, foxing, folds and coffee rings;
    - enamel signs with chips;
    - brushed and riveted metal plates;
    - wood with grain;
    - canvas and fabric with weave;
    - leather;
    - frosted glass;
    - painted hazard plates;
    - cork boards;
    - embroidered patches.
    Each screen picks its own material family. Paper is one option among many.
  - 3D vignettes only where they truly add something: a plant growing, the creature being fitted, the planet scan, the
    mastery dioramas. Don't put 3D in every panel.
  - NO TEXT SLOP: icons and pictures first, short labels, numbers where they matter, every word earning its place, no
    filler descriptions, no "click here" prose.
  - Composition: clean, beautiful, consistent margins, a clear hierarchy, nothing crowded, everything aligned to the
    pixel grid at integer scale.
  - INVENTORY: no 3D chest in the window or the HUD. It is a 2D pixel-art grid in the container's own material (a
    wooden crate gets a wood-and-rope frame, the cold store frosted steel, the soil pit an earthen frame, the tank a pipe
    gauge). Items are 2D sprites in cells. The bottom-left HUD shows 2D pixel-art container badges with a fill gauge and
    the top items.
- **ART FINISH (user):** hand-drawn strokes at high resolution, made with lowpix. 2D art (icons, frames, item art, UI
  doodles) uses lowpix's stroke brush engine (`--finish strokes`, hand-drawn outline curves, light laid in strokes that
  follow the form; see docs/SPRITES.md and lowpix/brush.py and painter.py). 3D models use lowpix `pixel-hd` textures with
  the same hand-drawn stroke feel. It should feel hand-drawn and hand-made everywhere.

## Story flow
Ship (intro) -> the scientist teaches gravity: you throw an apple, it punches a hole in the hull, the apple drifts into
space, the mechanic rushes to patch it -> she teaches building and repair -> you build your capsule pod in the hangar
-> the main monitor: scan the first galaxy, pick the easiest planet, pick a location, deploy (landing kept simple) ->
the planet colony loop -> return to the ship any time through the ship AI link.
